COOLING SOLUTIONS

Cooling Towers

A Cooling Tower is a heat rejection system designed to remove heat from industrial process water by transferring heat to atmospheric air through evaporation and direct contact between water and air.

Cooling towers are widely used in industrial plants, HVAC systems, power generation, and manufacturing processes.

At HESCO Water Integrated Systems, we design, manufacture, and supply cooling tower solutions using different international and local technologies.

Cooling Tower Operation Depends On

  1. Hot water enters the tower distribution system.
  2. Water flows through the fill material.
  3. Fans move air through the tower.
  4. Heat transfers from water to air through evaporation.
  5. Cooled water returns back to the process.

Cooling Tower Types

  • Open Circuit Cooling Towers
  • Closed Circuit Cooling Towers
  • Counter Flow Cooling Towers
  • Cross Flow Cooling Towers
  • FRP Cooling Towers
  • Galvanized Steel Cooling Towers

Chemical Cleaning & Descaling

Chemical Cleaning Services

Chemical cleaning is a controlled process used to remove accumulated scale, deposits, biological growth, corrosion products, oils, and other contaminants from water treatment and industrial equipment.

It Is Commonly Applied To

  • RO Membranes
  • Heat Exchangers
  • Cooling Towers
  • Chillers
  • Boilers
  • Filtration Systems
  • Industrial Piping

The Cleaning Process Generally Includes

01
System Inspection

Assessment of the equipment condition and identification of the type of fouling.

02
Chemical Selection

Selection of the appropriate cleaning chemical based on the contaminant and equipment material.

03
Preparation & Isolation

The equipment is isolated and connected to the chemical cleaning system.

04
Chemical Circulation

The cleaning solution is circulated through the equipment under controlled conditions.

05
Flushing

The system is thoroughly flushed with clean water to remove chemical residues.

06
Final Inspection & Testing

System performance is checked before returning the equipment to normal operation.

Water Softener Systems

Introduction

HESCO Water Integrated Systems provides engineered water softening solutions designed to reduce hardness-causing minerals, primarily calcium and magnesium, from water.

Our softener systems protect downstream equipment from scale formation and improve the efficiency and reliability of water treatment and industrial processes.

Applications

  • RO Pretreatment
  • Boiler Feed Water
  • Cooling Towers
  • Hot Water Systems
  • Hotels & Resorts

  • Laundry Systems
  • Food & Beverage Applications
  • General Utility Water

How Does a Water Softener Work?

Water passes through a cation exchange resin, where calcium and magnesium ions are exchanged with sodium ions. Once the resin reaches its capacity, the system performs a regeneration cycle using a brine solution to restore the resin's exchange capacity.

Raw Water → Softener Vessel → Soft Water

During Regeneration

Brine Preparation → Brine Injection → Slow Rinse → Fast Rinse → Service

MEMBRANE TECHNOLOGY

Ultrafiltration (UF) Systems

Introduction

HESCO Water Integrated Systems provides advanced Ultrafiltration (UF) systems for the removal of suspended solids, colloids, bacteria, and other high-molecular-weight contaminants from water.

UF technology uses membrane separation to produce consistent and high-quality filtered water, making it an effective solution for water pretreatment and water reuse applications.

Our UF systems can be designed as standalone treatment units or integrated with other treatment technologies such as Reverse Osmosis (RO), Multimedia Filtration, Activated Carbon Filtration, and other advanced treatment processes.

Applications

  • RO Pretreatment
  • Surface Water Treatment
  • Groundwater Treatment
  • Drinking Water Pretreatment
  • Wastewater Tertiary Treatment
  • Water Reuse
  • Industrial Process Water
  • Cooling Tower Makeup Water
  • Municipal Water Treatment
  • Pretreatment for High-Purity Water Systems

What Does Ultrafiltration Remove?

UF membranes provide an effective barrier against:

  • Suspended Solids (TSS)
  • Colloidal Particles
  • Turbidity
  • Bacteria
  • Microorganisms
  • High-Molecular-Weight Organic Matter
  • Fine Particles

UF is primarily a physical separation process and does not normally remove dissolved salts and low-molecular-weight dissolved substances. For applications requiring desalination, UF can be used as an effective pretreatment stage before Reverse Osmosis.

How Does Ultrafiltration Work?

During operation, feed water passes through UF membranes under pressure. Water and smaller dissolved components pass through the membrane as permeate, while suspended particles, colloids, and microorganisms are retained and removed through the concentrate stream.

Feed Water → UF Membranes → Permeate + Concentrate

Depending on the membrane configuration and application, the system can operate in different filtration modes and may include periodic backwashing and chemical cleaning to maintain membrane performance.

UF System Operation

A typical UF system may include several operating stages:

Filtration → Backwash → Air Scouring, where applicable → Rinse → Return to Service

Periodic Chemical Enhanced Backwash (CEB) and Clean-In-Place (CIP) procedures can also be incorporated according to membrane manufacturer requirements and feed-water characteristics.

Key Benefits

  • High and consistent filtration quality
  • Effective removal of suspended solids and colloids
  • Excellent turbidity reduction
  • Microbial barrier
  • Compact system footprint
  • Automated operation
  • Reduced chemical requirements compared with conventional clarification in suitable applications
  • Excellent pretreatment for RO systems
  • Suitable for water reuse applications

UF + RO Integration

UF + RO Integration

For applications requiring desalination, UF can be installed as an advanced pretreatment stage before RO. By reducing suspended solids, colloids, and turbidity in the RO feed water, UF can help provide more stable RO operation and reduce the risk of particulate and colloidal fouling.

Raw Water → Pretreatment → UF → Cartridge Filter → RO → Product Water
